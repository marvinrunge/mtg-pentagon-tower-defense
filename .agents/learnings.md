# Verified Project Learnings

Add only durable facts that were confirmed by a command or controlling code path.

- 2026-08-30: **The player animation library now stores every clip WHOLE.** The user opened the built scene and found almost every animation truncated - correctly, because `_trim_to_swing()` had been slicing each clip down to its strike window before saving, so `attack_light` was stored as a 0.63s stump of a 2.40s animation and the Mixamo return-to-rest was simply gone. Verified the project's source fbx files are byte-identical to the original "Pro Melee Axe Pack" download, so nothing was lost on import - the destruction was entirely in the builder.
  - Fixed by making the window DATA instead of a cut: `_measure_action_window()` records `trim_start` / `trim_length` / `hit_ratios` as metadata on the full clip, and `PlayerAnimator.play_action()` plays just that span through `AnimationNodeAnimation`'s custom timeline (`use_custom_timeline = true`, `start_offset`, `timeline_length`, `stretch_time_scale = false`). Confirmed every clip in `lib_player.tres` now matches its source length exactly, and that playback still plays the strike: hand speed peaks at 22.0/s during a swing against 0.65/s at idle, at 43% of the action.
  - `AnimationNodeAnimation` exposes `timeline_length`, `stretch_time_scale`, `start_offset` and `loop_mode` ONLY once `use_custom_timeline` is set - they do not appear in `get_property_list()` before that, so probing the class cold makes it look like there is no offset support (`stop_offset` genuinely does not exist; length is expressed as `timeline_length`).
  - General lesson: prefer recording a window as metadata over destructively re-authoring an imported asset. The asset stays inspectable and matches what was downloaded, and re-tuning the window becomes a value change rather than a rebuild of the source data.

- 2026-08-30: Fixed "many animations seem cut off", reported after a play test. Four separate causes, found by measuring rather than guessing - the useful metric is the tracked limb's speed at a clip's FIRST and LAST frame as a percentage of that clip's peak: a clip that starts or ends while the limb is still moving fast pops visibly.
  - **The dominant cause was `player_combo_window`, not the trimming.** At 0.5 a chained swing cut the previous one at exactly 50% - the impact frame - so every hit of a combo threw away its entire follow-through. Confirmed on frames: at the end of a chained swing's window the arm was still fully extended mid-strike, where an uninterrupted swing had returned to guard by then. Narrowed to 0.30, which lets ~70% of each swing play. It costs nothing in feel because `player_attack_buffer_time` already holds an early release until the window opens.
  - `_combo_reset_timer` was `_swing_duration() + player_combo_window`, i.e. adding a FRACTION to a value in seconds. Split out `player_combo_grace` (seconds) so narrowing the window no longer silently shortens the chain's grace period too.
  - The fixed +-0.4s SWING trim cut some clips mid-motion (kick ended at 41% of peak speed, cast_black at 42%, cast_white started at 36%). Replaced with `_settle_time()`, which walks outward from the impact until the limb drops to 25% of peak, clamped to [0.30, 0.60] either side. Every clip now measures under 35% at both boundaries (worst 33%, was 42%) AND the clips came out shorter, because the limbs settle sooner than 0.4s - so attacks dropped from 1.60x to 1.27x playback, which reads better as well.
  - The `jump` clip is 1.90s but the player is only airborne ~0.92s (2*jump_velocity/gravity), so it only ever showed 48% of itself. `PlayerAnimator._jump_speed_scale()` now fits the clip to the real hang time.
  - `player_anim_blend_action` 0.08 -> 0.12 so one swing chaining into the next reads as a blend rather than a snap.

- 2026-08-30 (Phase 3): Melee now feeds the spell kit - each melee impact frame that CONNECTS takes `GameSettings.melee_hit_cooldown_reduction` (0.35s) off every spell on cooldown, via `Player._reduce_spell_cooldowns()`. `_apply_melee_damage()` returns the number of enemies struck so a whiff refunds nothing. Two deliberate choices: the refund is paid per IMPACT FRAME, not per enemy struck (paying per enemy would make one finisher into a crowd reset the whole bar - a landed `combo_1` still refunds 5x = 1.75s from its five frames), and only ids `SpellDatabase.has_spell()` recognises are refunded, so the kick's own cooldown sitting in the same dictionary is never shortened by the kick's own impact.
  - Testing note: `spell_cooldown_timers` is also drained by the ordinary per-frame tick in `_physics_process`, so any assertion spanning a frame is off by 1/60s and reads as a failure. Measure the refund as a delta captured immediately either side of the call with no awaits in between; for end-to-end checks, compare the total drop against real wall time rather than an assumed elapsed figure.

- 2026-08-30 (Phase 2): Player casts became committed actions instead of instant keypress effects. `Player`'s melee state was renamed `_attack_* -> _action_*` and `_begin_action(clip, duration, upper_body, is_melee)` is now the shared primitive behind both a swing and a cast, with `_begin_melee_action()` layering the impact schedule on top. Facts worth keeping:
  - The five per-colour mage clips (`assets/animations/character/mage/attack_<colour>.fbx`) adopt onto the player rig MORE cleanly than the death clip does: every one of the player's 33 bones exists in the 57-bone source, and each clip carries exactly ONE position track, on Hips, so nothing has to be dropped. Trimmed with the same `Trim.SWING` policy as attacks, they come out 0.80s with the release frame measured at 50%.
  - **The cast release moment is NOT stored per spell.** It is a property of the animation, measured at build time and kept as `hit_ratios` metadata on the clip, read back through `PlayerAnimator.release_time()`. Several spells share one clip and all want the same moment, so a per-spell `release_ratio` field would have recreated exactly the duplication SpellDatabase was created to remove. `cast_duration` is the per-spell timing knob instead.
  - A cast's cooldown is stamped in `execute_spell()`, i.e. at the release frame, NOT when the button is pressed. That is what makes an interrupted cast free: `_cancel_action()` clears `_pending_cast_id` and the spell simply never happened. Verified that a stagger mid-wind-up leaves the spell off cooldown.
  - `_action_is_melee` exists so the combo window stays melee-only - without it a cast's tail would be chainable into a combo step.
  - Casting drops a raised guard for free: `_update_block()` already requires no action in flight, so committing to a cast lowers it on the next frame with no extra code.
  - **`node.global_position = x` BEFORE `add_child()` silently writes a LOCAL position** (and logs `Condition "!is_inside_tree()" is true`). Measured: with the eventual parent at the origin the result is accidentally correct, but with the parent offset by (100,0,100) the node lands 100 units out. Four spells did this - Rain of Ember, Chandra's Ignition, Hurricane and Toxic Deluge - and worked only because `get_tree().current_scene` sits at the origin. All four now add first and position second. Worth checking for elsewhere: it is invisible until something moves the scene root.

- 2026-08-30: Added `scripts/spell_database.gd` (`class_name SpellDatabase`, static accessors, NOT an autoload - same shape as `EnemyDatabase`/`BossDatabase`) as the single definition of all 25 player spells. It absorbed data that had been living in FIVE places that nothing kept in sync: `GameSettings.SPELL_COOLDOWNS`, `Player.is_chargeable()`'s hardcoded id list, `Player.get_spell_name_for_slot()`'s NAMES dict, and `SkillTree.SPELL_DATA` (names again, plus costs duplicating `GameSettings.TIER_COSTS`). `execute_spell()`'s 25-arm match stayed - the behaviour is genuinely bespoke, only the data was duplicated. Facts worth keeping:
  - Unlock costs are NOT stored per spell; they derive from `GameSettings.get_tier_cost(tier - 1)`. Duplicating them per row is how they drifted originally. A `static func` CAN reference an autoload singleton (`GameSettings`) in Godot 4 - verified at runtime, not just parsed.
  - The migration was checked against the pre-change sources extracted from `git show HEAD:...` rather than against notes: all 25 cooldowns, display names, chargeable flags and unlock costs reproduce exactly. Worth repeating for any future table consolidation - the whole risk of that kind of refactor is a silently altered value, and memory is not evidence.
  - `spell_cooldown_timers` holds non-spell entries too (the kick), so the Counterspell parry and Sign in Blood now call `_clear_spell_cooldowns()`, which erases only ids `SpellDatabase.has_spell()` recognises, instead of a blanket `clear()` that handed the kick back for free.
  - Number keys 1-5 now cast directly as well as selecting, mirroring `cast_spell` on both press AND release so a chargeable spell charges while the key is held and fires when it comes up - without the release branch, 1-5 could start a charge with no way to end it.

- 2026-08-30: Built the player's animated character (`tools/player_character_builder.gd` -> `scenes/misc/player_visual.tscn` + `assets/animations/player/lib_player.tres`, driven at runtime by `scripts/player_animator.gd`), replacing the `CSGCylinder3D` placeholder in `scenes/misc/player.tscn`. Facts worth keeping:
  - **`Skeleton3D.get_bone_global_pose()` returns the REST pose in a build-time script, no matter where the clip is seeked to.** It reads a cache that is only recomputed when the skeleton actually processes a frame, and a tool driving an `AnimationPlayer` by hand never gives it one. Confirmed directly: across a 2.4s swing, `get_bone_pose()` (local) varied per sample while `get_bone_global_pose()` returned one constant value for every sample, equal to the T-pose hand position. Compose the global pose from `get_bone_pose()` up the parent chain instead (`PlayerCharacterBuilder._bone_global_pose`), and set `callback_mode_process = ANIMATION_CALLBACK_MODE_PROCESS_MANUAL` plus `seek(t, true)` then `advance(0.0)` to make the mixer write the local poses at all. **This means `_measure_min_foot_height()` in `tools/character_builder.gd` and `tools/boss_character_builder.gd` has been silently measuring the rest pose rather than each clip's own lowest foot** - their per-clip ground correction is effectively one constant correction, not per-clip. Not fixed here (would change every enemy/boss scene's grounding and none of them look wrong today), but it is the reason the player builder does its own composition.
  - The player rig (`assets/player/Meshy_AI_Orc_Warrior_in_Fur_Ar_0829200310_texture.fbx`) is at **metric scale, ~1.905 units head-to-toe** - unlike every enemy/boss/myr mesh in this project (~0.02 units, blown up 85-180x). Its root scale correction onto the 1.9-tall player capsule is ~0.9976x. Measured from the skeleton's own bone rests, NOT the mesh AABB: this mesh's skin binds into a differently-scaled vertex space, so `ArrayMesh.get_aabb()` reports a meaningless 0.0044 units tall with its longest axis on Z.
  - That rigged fbx has **UVs but no texture data and no vertex colours** - it carries FBX `Video` nodes with no image payload and no external path, so nothing is extracted. Its PBR maps therefore come from a SECOND Meshy export of the same model (`...0830103939_texture{,_normal,_metallic,_roughness}.png`, all 2048x2048), while the rig comes from the first (`...0829200310`). **Mixing exports like this is safe here and was verified, not assumed**: both are the same 24014-vertex / 20750-triangle mesh, and after corresponding them triangle-by-triangle (hash each triangle on its three sorted quantised corner positions - a bare position is ambiguous at a UV seam, a triangle is not) all 62250 UV corners are identical to the last decimal. Only the vertex ORDER was shuffled by the re-export (just 683/24014 positions and 665/24014 normals line up in place), which is exactly what a texture lookup does not care about. Comparing UVs index-by-index across two such exports is meaningless and will report a spurious mismatch - correspond first. Confirmed by render: skin, armour, face and straps all land correctly.
  - **These Mixamo rigs face +Z, and the player needed a 180-degree yaw on its VISUAL because of it.** Measured, not guessed: `standing walk forward` carries its hips from z = -0.006 to z = +1.318, and a render from the +Z side shows the character's face. Godot's forward is -Z, so the first in-game test had the player staring into the camera parked behind them and moonwalking. `EnemyBase` solves the identical mismatch from the other end - it turns with `atan2(dir.x, dir.z)` and `enemy_base.gd` documents "+Z is forward for these enemies" - but that option is closed for `Player`, whose -Z forward is already baked into the camera rig, `cast_basic_attack`'s cone, `_blocks_attack_from`'s cone and knockback. `PlayerCharacterBuilder.FACING_CORRECTION_DEGREES` bakes the yaw into `player_visual.tscn`'s root instead, which touches nothing else: the yaw is a rigid transform of the whole rig, and `PlayerAnimator`'s clip picking reads the Animator node's basis (unrotated), not the visual's. Verified afterwards that all six locomotion clips still map to the right direction - walk/run forward -> body dz -1.32/-1.81, back -> +1.26/+1.52, left -> body dx -1.16, right -> +1.34 - i.e. the yaw did not mirror the strafes.
  - Provenance of the two export ids, per the user (2026-08-30): `0829200310` (the rigged one) was run through **Blender** because Meshy's direct export was not accepted by Mixamo; `0830103939` is the **direct** Meshy fbx export. That is the Blender round-trip showing up as the vertex reordering measured above, and it is why the split is permanent rather than something to tidy up later - the rig can only come from the laundered export (the one Mixamo accepted) and the textures can only come from the direct one (the laundered one lost its image payload). Note this does NOT explain the +Z facing: the animation clips never went through Blender and face +Z too, so re-exporting the mesh with a different forward axis would not fix facing.
  - The second export's zip also contains an fbx, but it is the **un-rigged** Meshy model (`Node3D > Mesh_0`, no `Skeleton3D`, no `AnimationPlayer`) - the rig only exists in the Mixamo-processed first export, so the mesh source cannot simply be swapped to the newer file.
  - The emission map Meshy ships is black in practice (sampled max 0.027 across the image) and is deliberately not wired into the material.
  - The 48 clips under `assets/player/` and the mesh share the same 33-bone rig with **identical bone offsets** (`dpos = 0`), but rest *rotations* differ by up to 50.77 degrees on `LeftArm`/`RightArm` (A-pose mesh vs T-pose clips). That difference is harmless - verified by rendering idle/walk/run/block/attack/death poses, all of which read correctly with no twisted limbs - so no retarget correction of the kind `melee_character_builder.gd` once needed is applied here.
  - Mixamo attack clips are 2.3-4.7s of neutral-stance lead-in and recovery against a 0.5s attack cadence. `_trim_to_swing()` measures the striking limb's speed profile and cuts each clip down (single strikes to a 0.8s window around the impact frame; the `combo_*` flourishes to their whole working span, 1.45-3.05s), and records every impact moment as `metadata/hit_ratios` on the clip so gameplay lands damage on the hit frame rather than on keypress. `metadata/` survives `ResourceSaver.save()` into the `.tres`, but `Animation.duplicate()` drops it - carry it across by hand.
  - `scripts/player_animator.gd` runs on an **AnimationTree built in code**, not on bare `AnimationPlayer.play()` calls, because a light attack has to play on the upper body while the legs keep walking - two clips on one skeleton, which one AnimationPlayer cannot do. Graph: per-clip `AnimationNodeAnimation` -> `AnimationNodeTransition` (locomotion cross-fade) -> `AnimationNodeTimeScale` (stride matching) into input 0 of an `AnimationNodeOneShot`, with the action clip -> its own `TimeScale` into input 1. The OneShot carries a bone filter of every bone from the spine up (derived from the skeleton as "not in LOWER_BODY_BONES", so a rig change cannot silently unmask one); `filter_enabled` is toggled per fire - on for a light attack (upper body only), off for anything full-body. Hips MUST stay on the locomotion side: it carries the vertical bob and the builder's ground correction. API facts confirmed by probe on 4.7: `AnimationNodeTransition` inputs are created by setting `input_count` then `input_<n>/name` / `input_<n>/reset` (reset defaults TRUE - leave it true for one-shot-ish states like death/jump/idle_look, false for the loops or every walk/run swap restarts the stride); `AnimationNodeOneShot` has `filter_enabled` + `set_filter_path()`; `ONE_SHOT_REQUEST_FIRE == 1`. The AnimationTree is given the library directly (`add_animation_library`) with `root_node = ".."`, and the AnimationPlayer is `stop()`ped so two mixers aren't writing the same bones. Death rides the LOCOMOTION transition rather than the one-shot, because a one-shot fades back out at the end and a corpse has to hold its final pose.
  - Full-body player clips always pair with rooting the character (`Player.UPPER_BODY_ATTACKS` lists the only exception, the light attack): a full-body clip over a character still sliding along the floor is the foot-sliding bug the layering exists to remove, so heavy attacks, finishers, kicks and staggers zero horizontal velocity. Zero it OUTRIGHT - falling through to the existing `move_toward(velocity.x, 0, current_speed)` with `current_speed = 0` is a no-op and the player coasts on at the velocity the swing started with. Verified by measuring bone-pose change rates rather than by eye: walking baseline was RightUpLeg 2.85 rad/s / RightForeArm 0.71 rad/s, and during a light attack RightUpLeg stayed at 2.14 rad/s (legs still walking) while RightForeArm hit 12.76 rad/s (18x the walk's own arm sway, i.e. genuinely driven by the swing). Checking only "both bones moved" would have passed even with the filter doing nothing, since the walk cycle swings the arms too.
  - Player melee swings fire on the attack button's **release**, not its press (changed 2026-08-30 after the first play test). Firing light on press and then swapping the clip to heavy once the hold threshold passed visibly cut the light animation off mid-stroke. Release-driven also deletes a whole class of problem: nothing is in flight while the button is down, so the old `_update_heavy_upgrade()`, the `_swing_upgraded` flag, the "must stay below the light swing's impact frame" constraint on `player_heavy_hold_time`, and the guard against re-arming an already-paid-out hit are all gone. A resolved-but-unstartable swing is buffered for `player_attack_buffer_time` so a release landing just before the combo window opens still connects.
  - **Check `is_action_just_pressed` and `is_action_just_released` independently, never as an if/elif chain.** A click fast enough to open and close inside one physics frame reports BOTH as just-happened; an elif takes the press branch, never sees the release, and silently eats the input - the button is already up so no release event ever arrives again. Found by a test that pressed and released in the same frame; the fix resolves it as a zero-length hold, i.e. a light tap.
  - Testing player input in Godot: `Input.action_press/release()` called from a coroutine that resumed on the `physics_frame` signal lands AFTER that frame's `_physics_process`, so the action is not observed until the frame **after next** - await two physics frames before asserting, or checks fail one frame early and look like real bugs. Also note `_update_melee()` ignores all input unless `Input.mouse_mode == MOUSE_MODE_CAPTURED`, which a `--headless` run cannot satisfy: input-driven player tests must run WINDOWED (`--resolution 640x400 --quit-after N`), otherwise every input branch is skipped and checks pass vacuously.
  - Player controls were reworked with the character: LMB tap = light attack / LMB hold past `player_heavy_hold_time` upgrades that same swing to heavy, RMB hold = block, F = kick, and `cast_spell` moved RMB->Q (gamepad LT->RB) and `interact` moved F->E (gamepad X unchanged) to free them. The recorded light/heavy symbols of the first two swings pick the finisher the third press launches (`LL`->combo_1, `LH`->combo_2, `HH`->combo_3, else `attack_finish`) - the third press's own weight can't be known at the moment it has to start an animation, so it is only the trigger. `player_heavy_hold_time` must stay below the light swing's own impact frame or the upgrade would re-arm a hit that already paid out; `_update_heavy_upgrade()` guards on `_pending_hits` being non-empty rather than trusting the tuning.
  - The standing pack ships no death clip; `_adopt_foreign_clip()` borrows `assets/animations/character/common/death_fallback.fbx` from the ENEMY rig (57 bones, ~96x smaller). Rotation tracks transfer by bone name unchanged; every position track except Hips must be DROPPED (they would move this rig's joints to enemy-scale offsets and collapse it), and the Hips track rescaled by the measured rig-height ratio. Verified by render: the player lies on the ground with correct proportions.
  - A `--script` SceneTree run does **not** register autoload singletons, so any test touching `scripts/player.gd` (which references `GameSettings` at file scope) fails to compile there with "Identifier not found: GameSettings". Run such tests as a scene (`godot --headless --path . tools/whatever.tscn`) instead. Separately, a `--script` run does not rescan the project either, so a newly added `class_name` is not in `.godot/global_script_class_cache.cfg` until an editor session picks it up - `tools/build_player_character.gd` therefore `preload()`s the builder by path rather than naming its global class.
  - Adding a node to `get_tree().get_root()` from the current scene's own `_ready()` silently fails ("Parent node is busy setting up children"). Await one `process_frame` first.

  - It also CANCELS the swing in flight (`_impact_timer = -1.0`), because damage is scheduled separately from the animation now - without that the enemy would flinch and still land the hit it was in the middle of. `enemy_hit_react_interrupts_attack` turns that half off and leaves flinches purely cosmetic.

- 2026-09-02: **Rooting and body coverage are now two separate questions, not one.** `Player._begin_action()` used to derive `_action_roots_player` from `upper_body`, on the reasoning that a full-body clip over a sliding character is exactly the foot-sliding the bone filter exists to remove. Titanic Leap is the case where that reasoning inverts: it moves the caster AND needs its own legs, because the movement it makes IS the jump the clip is playing. `_begin_action(clip, duration, upper_body, roots, is_melee, window)` now takes both, `SpellDatabase` gained an optional `upper_body` field defaulting to `not roots`, and melee still ties them together at its own call site.
  - Watch the argument that used to read `_commit_action(duration, false, true)` in `_release_heavy_charge()`: `false` meant "not upper body" and therefore "roots". Under the new signature the same literal means "does NOT root" - it had to flip to `true`.
- 2026-09-02: **A light attack started standing still is a different swing from one started on the move.** Standing plays the chain clip WHOLE - its own footwork included - and holds the player there until it finishes; moving masks the same swing to the upper body and lets the walk cycle carry on underneath. Blade Dance's third stage is planted full-body footwork with no walking version, so a player on the move never reaches it and the chain restarts instead.
  - `_wants_to_move()` reads INPUT, not velocity. A standing swing roots the character, so by the time the next stage is due their velocity is zero whatever they are holding, and a chain that began standing would lock itself into standing until it lapsed.
  - Verified all four combinations headlessly: standing gives roots=true/filter=false at every stage and reaches stage 2 at x1.5 damage once Blade Dance is on; moving gives roots=false/filter=true and wraps stage 2 back to 0.

- 2026-09-02: **The camera shake had never been visible: a SpringArm3D rewrites its direct children's transforms every frame.** `Player.setup_camera()` parented the Camera3D straight to the SpringArm3D, and the arm parks each direct child at `(0, 0, current_spring_length)` so collision can pull it in - so every offset `_update_camera_shake()` wrote onto `camera.position` was wiped before it could be drawn. Proved it directly: wrote `(0.5, 0.5, 0)` onto the camera, and one frame later it read `(0, 0, 3.2)` - X and Y zeroed, Z replaced by the spring length. The old comment claiming the camera "otherwise sits at the origin of its SpringArm3D" was simply wrong.
  - Fixed with a `ShakePivot` Node3D between the two: the arm rewrites the pivot, and the camera hangs off it with nothing but the shake touching its own local position. Verified end to end after the fix - a requested 0.09 peaks at 0.062 of camera offset, 0.75 peaks at 0.836, and every shake settles back to exactly 0.
  - The peak lands slightly under the requested strength for short shakes and slightly over for long ones, because the amplitude decays as `falloff * falloff` while the two axes are sampled at different frequencies; requested strength is a scale, not a measured maximum.

- 2026-09-02: **Fire is two particle systems with different blend modes, and the colour never lives in the texture.** `scripts/ember_fx.gd` builds every red/green effect from one white-on-transparent puff texture and one white-on-transparent radial glow (`assets/vfx/`, placeholders from `tools/build_vfx_textures.gd`). All colour arrives as the particle system's `color_ramp` reaching the quad as a VERTEX COLOUR, which is why the material must set `vertex_color_use_as_albedo` - and why one texture serves an orange firestorm, a red fireball and any future blue or green spell. `Projectile` swaps in a two-stop ramp of the bolt's own colour without touching the mesh.
  - The body is large/soft/slow and ALPHA-blended; the sparks are small/hard/fast and ADDITIVE. Not interchangeable: a fully additive fire has no darks and turns into a white blob where it overlaps itself, and fully alpha-blended sparks never look hot.
  - Settings that actually mattered: POSITIVE Y gravity on the flame body (fire falls up - negative gravity makes a rising column arc back down and read as debris); `BILLBOARD_PARTICLES` plus `billboard_keep_scale` rather than `BILLBOARD_ENABLED`, which throws away each particle's own rotation and scale and leaves a column looking like a stack of identical stamps; `DEPTH_DRAW_DISABLED`, since these are always translucent and always overlapping; a scale curve that comes back DOWN after its peak, so a puff reads as being consumed; and a deliberately jagged spark scale curve bouncing between ~1.0 and ~0.1 several times per life, which is what reads as blinking where a smooth fade reads as a small soft dot.
  - The usual recipe keyframes an `AnimationPlayer` for the fire light's energy. Driven from code instead (`EmberFx.flicker`, two sines at unrelated frequencies): identical at a glance, but it never repeats over any window the eye can latch onto, and needs no resource per effect. Measured 2.12-3.87 around a base of 3.0.
  - Full reference, including what to pull from the Textures-for-VFX database and how to add a new effect: `docs/VFX_TEXTURES.md`.

- 2026-09-02: **A SPAN trim ends 0.20s after the limb stops moving, which throws away any RECOVERY that is part of the move.** `jump_attack` was the case that exposed it: its window ended at 1.97s of a 3.67s clip - 0.20s past the slam, i.e. mid-crouch on the landing - so the character snapped back to idle without ever standing up. Measured the whole clip in 0.2s buckets to confirm nothing large was left (hips peak at the leap 0.8-1.0s and the slam 1.4-1.6s, everything after 1.8s under 10%), but that thin tail IS the stand-up and is exactly what read as cut off.
  - Fixed with `SPAN_LEAD_OUT_OVERRIDE` in the player builder, per clip, rather than by raising `SPAN_LEAD_OUT` for everything - a combo flourish genuinely does want its tail cut. The window is clamped to the clip length, so a generous value just means "to the end".
  - That makes the animation 2.95s instead of 1.25s, and the fix for the lockout it would otherwise cause is a new optional `commit` field in `SpellDatabase`: how long the player is actually tied up, when that is SHORTER than the animation. `_begin_cast` now calls `_commit_action()` and `animator.play_action()` separately instead of going through `_begin_action`, the same split `_release_heavy_charge` already uses. Retune cast_duration and the window together or the arc silently desyncs.

- 2026-09-03: **The economy is three currencies with three owners, and the split is what removes every division problem.** XP is shared across the team and levels arrive for everyone simultaneously (no last-hit, no kill-stealing, nobody left behind); SKILL POINTS are personal and buy the tree; MANA is a shared team pool, still coloured, banked automatically on kill and spent only at Upkeep on team purchases. Skill points answer "what do I want to play", mana answers "what do we need to survive", so a player never chooses between their build and the team's. `scripts/run_state.gd` (autoload) owns all of it - an autoload rather than `MainController` state because a singleton is far easier to make server-authoritative than scene state reached through `get_tree().current_scene`.

  - `wave_rest_period` (3s) is replaced by `upkeep_duration` (30s). The wave loop now runs `_trigger_next_wave()` -> `upkeep_started` -> the panel -> `upkeep_finished` -> `start_next_wave()`; nothing else may emit `upkeep_finished`, because it is what actually starts the wave.
  - Consensus at Upkeep rises with permanence: myrs and skill-points-for-everyone go through on any player's click, enchantments need a majority of connected players. Proposing RESERVES the mana rather than spending it, so two open proposals cannot overdraw the pool.

- 2026-09-03: **Phases 2-4 of the multiplayer plan are in; the split that makes it cheap is "replicate the consequence, not the cause".** Enemies, waves, the crystal, the economy and the Upkeep vote are all the server's; every client keeps its own instant feedback and gives up only the authority to decide numbers.
  - **Enemies go through the same `spawn_function` trap the players did.** `enemy.setup(data)` used to run after `add_child` on the server only, and `set_meta("target_crystal"/"elite_modifier")` never crossed. `MainController._spawn_enemy` now takes colour/type/elite/position in the spawn argument and `EnemyBase._ready` rebuilds the same `EnemyData` locally - a Resource cannot replicate, but the two strings that identify it can.   - **Damage is a REQUEST.** `EnemyBase.request_damage(amount, attacker_peer, is_melee)` is an `any_peer` RPC the server applies; `Player._deal_damage()` calls straight through on the server and RPCs otherwise. `attacker_peer` rather than a node path, because paths are fragile across peers and attribution matters - lifesteal and the melee-into-spell cooldown refund both pay whoever landed the blow.
  - **Actions replicate as INTENT.** `Player._net_play_action(clip, duration, upper_body, window)` costs five values on the wire, and each peer's own `PlayerAnimator` reconstructs the pose from the clip metadata it already has. Never send a skeleton.
  - **Spell effects need an authority even though the cast does not.** A projectile spawned on a client is invisible to everyone else and its damage never reaches the server's enemies. `execute_spell()` therefore runs the cooldown and animation locally but RPCs `_run_spell_effect` to the server, and `Projectile._on_body_entered` deals damage only on the server.

Levels are the exception - rare, important, and sent immediately. Verified across two peers: the host killing 5 enemies produced 50 XP and 3 White/2 Blue on both.
  - **Wave SIZE never scaled with head count, only enemy damage did.** Five players met a solo-sized wave, shredded it without the crystal being threatened, and earned solo-sized income. `GameSettings.get_wave_size_factor()` now multiplies the wave budget - 1.45x at two players, 2.8x at five.

- 2026-09-03: **Enemy DAMAGE is the worst of the three difficulty levers to scale with head count; count is the best and health is the correction.** The project scaled damage from 0.2x solo to 1.0x at five players, and adding wave-size scaling on top produced **14x the incoming damage at five players versus solo** - a straight double-count, since both levers were delivering the same solo assist.
  - Damage is the only lever that changes what a hit COSTS. At 0.2 a Cleric's swing was worth 20% solo and 100% in a full team, so a player could never learn what any attack is actually worth - it depended on how many friends had logged in. It also punishes the individual for the team growing, because with five players you are usually alone in your own lane facing enemies that now hit five times harder.
  - **Count is the right primary lever for THIS game** because it is the only one that uses the map: more enemies means more of the five lanes under real pressure at once, which is the entire reason a five-player team exists. Every enemy still feels exactly as it does solo.
  - **Health is the correction count cannot make.** Count scaling assumes players SPREAD OUT, one per lane, each killing at solo speed. The moment they group - a boss, a collapsing lane - five players focus-fire and delete each enemy about five times faster, and more enemies does not help with that. Kept modest (1.6x at five) because health is the lever that makes things spongy, and melee suffers most since every swing is a committed animation.
  - Settled on damage 0.75->1.0, wave size 1.0->2.8x, enemy health 1.0->1.6x. That is 3.7x incoming and 4.5x health to chew at five players, against a team bringing roughly 5x the dps and 5x the bodies. Needs a real playtest, but it is proportional rather than a 14x cliff.

- 2026-09-03: **Crystal maximum health should NOT scale with player count, and removing the wave rewards had silently left no way to heal it at all.** Crystal repair was one of the three "battle boons"; deleting that screen took the game's only repair with it, so from that commit until now every point of leaked damage was permanent for the whole run.
  - Scaling the crystal's maximum is the wrong fix for the same reason scaling enemy damage was: the crystal is ONE SHARED objective, not a per-player resource. Five players do not get five crystals, they get five defenders for the same one - so raising its health makes each leak matter LESS exactly when the team has more people available to prevent it, and it makes "the crystal has 1000" unlearnable.
  - The right lever is a PURCHASE. `upkeep_crystal_repair_amount` (200) for `upkeep_crystal_repair_cost` (8 colourless) at Upkeep, offered only when something is actually missing. It scales on its own - more players earn more mana, so more repair is affordable - and the trade against an enchantment stack is the interesting decision. Any-player rather than a vote, because in an emergency you want whoever notices to be able to act.
  - Verified: 450 damage, three repairs restore 200/200/50 and stop at the maximum, and a repair with nothing missing charges nothing.
