# VFX Textures

Reference for building spell and impact effects in this project. The working example
is `scripts/ember_fx.gd`, which every red and green spell effect is built from.

## Source

**Textures for VFX Database** — <https://simonschreibt.notion.site/Textures-for-VFX-Database-2c72eccccfa84a0eae927d778ad746cc>

A curated index of texture packs and generators for real-time VFX, maintained by
Simon Schreibt. Use it before generating anything by hand: the shapes that read as
fire, smoke, lightning and impacts are well-explored, and a good authored noise puff
beats anything a builder script can produce.

### What to take from it

Look for **greyscale, white-on-transparent** assets. Anything that ships with baked
colour is much less useful here, for the reason under "Colour lives in the gradient"
below. The entries worth searching for, in rough order of usefulness to this project:

| Need | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Flame body, smoke, explosions | Turbulent puff / "explosion" sheets. One frame is enough — this project does not use flipbooks. |
| Sparks, glints, light points | Soft radial falloff, sometimes called "glow" or "point". |
| Lightning, arcs | Branching streak textures, for a blue or white spell. |
| Slashes, shockwaves | Crescent and ring shapes, for a melee or slam effect. |
| Distortion | Normal / flow maps, if a heat-haze shader ever gets written. |

Several linked generators export a single PNG from an "explosion" preset. When using
one: **disable its built-in colours**, raise emission and bloom, and export white on
transparent.

## The two textures here

Both live in `assets/vfx/` and are **placeholders**, generated by
`tools/build_vfx_textures.gd`. Overwrite them in place, keep the filenames, and
nothing else has to change.

| File | Shape | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| `fire_smoke.png` | Turbulent round puff | Flame bodies — the mass of a fire |
| `spark_glow.png` | Soft radial falloff | Sparks, falling embers, points of light |

256×256 is plenty. These are drawn small, often dozens at a time, and a 512 or 1024
texture costs memory and bandwidth for detail nobody will ever resolve.

## What this implementation established

### Colour lives in the gradient, not in the texture

Every texture here is white on transparent, and all colour comes from the particle
system's `color_ramp` reaching the quad as a **vertex colour** (which is why the
material sets `vertex_color_use_as_albedo`). One puff texture therefore serves an
orange firestorm, a red fireball and any blue or green spell added later. A texture
with baked colour can only ever be the one effect it was painted as.

`Projectile` uses this directly: the fireball keeps the full fire gradient, and every
other bolt swaps in a two-stop ramp of its own colour without touching the mesh.

### Fire is two systems, not one

This is most of what makes it read as fire rather than as orange confetti:

- **Body** — large, soft, slow, **alpha-blended** puffs that grow as they rise and
  cool from bright orange through red to dark grey.
- **Sparks** — small, hard, fast, **additively blended** points on a deliberately
  jagged scale curve.

The blend modes are not interchangeable. A fully additive fire has no darks in it and
turns into a white blob wherever it overlaps itself; fully alpha-blended sparks never
look hot.

### Specific settings that mattered

- **Positive Y gravity** on the flame body. Fire falls *up* — with negative gravity a
  rising column arcs back down and reads as debris.
- **`BILLBOARD_PARTICLES`**, not `BILLBOARD_ENABLED`, plus `billboard_keep_scale`.
  The plain mode throws away each particle's own rotation and scale, and a column of
  identically-oriented quads reads as a stack of stamps. Random `angle_min/max` on
  top of that is what stops repetition being visible.
- **`depth_draw_mode = DEPTH_DRAW_DISABLED`.** These are always translucent and
  always overlapping themselves; depth writes buy nothing and cost fill rate.
- **A scale curve that comes back down.** Growing to a peak around two thirds through
  and thinning after reads as being consumed; growing monotonically does not.
- **A jagged spark scale curve.** Bouncing between ~1.0 and ~0.1 several times across
  one particle's life is what reads as *blinking*. A smooth fade just reads as a
  small soft dot.
- **Alpha carries the fade, not brightness.** The gradient's last stops drop alpha to
  zero rather than only darkening, or the puff dissolves into a visible grey disc.

### Flickering light

A fire wants an `OmniLight3D` whose energy never sits still. The common recipe is an
`AnimationPlayer` with keyframed `Energy` set to autoplay; this project drives it from
code instead (`EmberFx.flicker`), summing two sine waves at unrelated frequencies.
Both look the same at a glance, but the code version never repeats over any window the
eye can latch onto, whereas a looping keyframe track eventually does — and it needs no
resource on disk per effect. Measured range around a base of 3.0: **2.12 – 3.87**.

### Budget

This is a tower defence. A late wave can have a firestorm burning while several
fireballs are in flight, on machines that already needed a graphics-options menu to
run at all. Current counts: trail 28, ground fire 54, ember rain 70, burst 40 + 36.
Treat those as ceilings rather than starting points.

## Adding a new effect

1. Pick a shape from the database; export white on transparent; drop it in
   `assets/vfx/`.
2. Add a builder to `scripts/ember_fx.gd` (or a sibling module for a different
   element) returning a configured `GPUParticles3D`.
3. Build the material with `EmberFx.particle_mesh(size, texture, additive)` so the
   billboard, blending and vertex-colour settings stay consistent.
4. Colour it with a `color_ramp`, never with the texture.
5. If it should light the world, add `build_fire_light()` and tick `flicker()`.
